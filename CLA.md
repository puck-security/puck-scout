# Puck Security Contributor License Agreement

Thank you for your interest in contributing to Puck.  Before we can accept
your code, you (or your employer) need to sign this Contributor License
Agreement (CLA).  The CLA gives Puck Security, Inc. the rights it needs to
distribute and sublicense your contribution as part of the project — while
making clear that **you retain copyright in your contribution.**

This CLA is adapted from the Apache Software Foundation's well-known
Individual and Corporate CLA templates (v2.2), which are used by hundreds
of commercial-OSS projects.  We did not invent novel terms; if you have
signed an Apache-style CLA before, this will look familiar.

There are two versions below.  Sign whichever applies:

- **[Individual CLA](#individual-contributor-license-agreement)** — if you
  are contributing as an individual, on your own time, with no employer
  claim to your work.
- **[Corporate CLA](#corporate-contributor-license-agreement)** — if you
  are contributing as part of your employment, or your employer has any
  rights in the work you contribute.  A signatory authorised to bind the
  company must sign this.

When you open your first pull request, the [CLA Assistant](https://github.com/contributor-assistant/github-action)
bot will comment with a link to sign.  Signing is a one-line comment on
the PR; the bot records signatures in
[`signatures/version1/cla.json`](signatures/version1/cla.json) in this
repository.

## How to sign

When you open your first PR, the CLA bot will comment with the exact
text to paste.  Today that line is:

> I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA

Signing happens entirely in the PR — no PDFs, no DocuSign, no separate
account.  Your signature is recorded with your GitHub username + the
commit SHA you signed against, and stored in
[`signatures/version1/cla.json`](signatures/version1/cla.json) for
auditability.
